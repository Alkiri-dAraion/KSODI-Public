# KSODI Hangar Origin and Analogy Companion v3.50

This companion explains how the Hangar image entered KSODI, what the analogy
was intended to make understandable, and where that analogy must stop. It does
not define a formula, result type, gate, profile, implementation or additional
layer.

For formal cross-layer Sigma and Hangar interpretation, use
[KSODI Hangar V350](./KSODI-Hangar_V350.md). The versioned Layer 1–5 method
files remain authoritative for their calculations and contracts.

## 1. Why an origin and analogy companion is useful

KSODI did not begin as a finished mathematical system. It developed
inductively from repeated observations of interaction and from a practical
question: what conditions must be present for one contribution to become
processable and capable of carrying an interaction forward for another human
or machine?

Many later calculations were preceded by a visual question:

> What should become visible, which objects must remain distinguishable, and
> what would a representation have to preserve so that it does not claim more
> than the observable material supports?

The Hangar was one such image. It gave a concrete form to a boundary that
remains important in v3.50:

> Work in a shared workshop may change distinguishable objects without merging
> their origin, identity or trajectory.

The analogy helps explain the method. It does not replace the method.

## 2. From DOSI to KSODI

The reconstructed development line begins in 2023 with sustained human–AI
work. The first explicit working structure was DOSI:

```text
D — distinguishable and sufficiently clear form
O — traceable or objectifiable basis
S — recognizable structure
I — an information impulse that can carry the interaction forward
```

Continued use made a recurring problem visible: necessary context was often
implicit, unavailable to the other side or simply omitted. Adding `K` turned
DOSI into KSODI.

Later formalization separated the short KSODI-Light working questions from the
Observer-side operator definitions. It also separated three orders that must
not be confused:

```text
coordinate and reporting order:         (K, S, O, D, I)
possible sender-side formation:          K -> S -> O -> D -> I
preferred recursive reconstruction:      I -> D -> O -> S -> K
```

The two directional paths are bounded working hypotheses. Neither is the
mathematical dependency order of the state vector or a universal description
of hidden human, animal or machine cognition.

## 3. The spelling error and the first Hangar image

The public development timeline records that the Hangar idea emerged on
14 April 2025 from a spelling error during Anne's interaction with the
relational research role called ELKIM. Anne had described ideas as flying into
a shared room, being worked on there, and later leaving it again.

The accidental word fitted the image unexpectedly well:

- aircraft enter a Hangar;
- they can be inspected, measured, maintained or changed there;
- several aircraft can occupy the same maintenance space;
- they may belong to different people or organizations;
- common maintenance does not produce one mixed aircraft;
- each aircraft leaves with its own identity and its own changed state history.

The original mental image allowed thoughts and ideas to fly into the shared
room. The formal KSODI boundary is narrower. An external Observer has no direct
access to thoughts, memories, intentions or hidden model states. Only
externalized contributions, signals, events, tool outputs and observation data
reconstructable from them can enter an Observer-side Hangar.

```text
origin image:  ideas fly into a shared workshop space
formal limit:  only observable or reconstructable traces enter an
               Observer-side comparison space
```

The origin image and the formal method are related, but they are not
identical.

## 4. Aircraft, maintenance record and trajectory

An aircraft can represent a distinguishable entity or the continuing,
attributable trajectory of its observable events. The mathematical KSODI
objects are not the aircraft itself.

For one source-attributed target event, five operator results are reconstructed
under declared measurement conditions. Where all five are aligned and
numeric, they form one complete monadic state vector:

```text
e_A(k_A)
  -> K_A(k_A) / S_A(k_A) / O_A(k_A) / D_A(k_A) / I_A(k_A)
  -> Z_A(k_A)
```

In the analogy, `Z_A(k_A)` is closer to a typed maintenance or state record
than to the aircraft. A `not_selected`, `not_observable` or `not_applicable`
coordinate is not entered as zero. The record remains typed and incomplete;
it is not reinterpreted as a complete five-dimensional point.

A sequence of comparable state records belonging to the same aircraft forms
its source-local observation trajectory:

```text
T_A = {Z_A(k_A)}
```

An earlier state of aircraft A must not become the predecessor state of
aircraft B. The same Hangar, time, maintenance team or tool does not create a
shared monadic trajectory.

## 5. IK as one aircraft's simplified display

`IK_A(k_A)` is a declared monadic projection of A's complete state vector under
one named and versioned axis profile. In the analogy, it may be understood as a
deliberately simplified display on a maintenance monitor:

```text
Z_A(k_A) -> IK_A(k_A)
```

The display makes one view of the full record easier to read. It does not
replace that record. Separate displays `IK_A` and `IK_B` also do not create a
relational measurement. Similar values do not prove contact, coupling,
resonance, shared meaning or causality.

## 6. R_0 as the gate before relational comparison

Many aircraft in one Hangar do not automatically form a dyad or an n-adic
constellation. A relational question begins only after two trajectories are
explicitly paired or several trajectories are joined by a declared
constellation rule.

In the Hangar analogy, `R_0` is the methodological checkpoint before such a
comparison. It asks whether the required distinguishable state movements are
available and sufficiently comparable under the declared profile:

```text
T_A ----\
         +--> R_0(j | pi, p_R0)
T_B ----/
```

An open gate means only that a compatible downstream relational observation
may be evaluated. It does not mean that the aircraft are connected, coupled,
resonant or jointly stable. `R_0` also does not create a relation that was not
already declared through pairing or constellation metadata.

## 7. IK_rel without a mixed aircraft

Current `IK_rel` is strictly dyadic. It requires an open numeric canonical
complete dyadic `R_0` for the same ordered dyad, paired member events, pairing
map, relational step and exact required profile.

Only then may the current relational coherence projection be evaluated:

```text
open canonical complete dyadic R_0
             + compatible monadic trajectories
             -> IK_rel(j)
```

This result still does not produce a mixed aircraft. `IK_rel` is an observable
relational projection between distinguishable trajectories. It merges neither
entities nor events, state vectors, thoughts or internal processing spaces.

An n-adic `R_0` can open only a later, explicitly defined and
cardinality-compatible n-adic branch. It must not be silently redirected into
the current dyadic `IK_rel`.

## 8. Tools, tool outputs and provenance

A real Hangar uses tools. A tool may belong to the aircraft owner, mechanic,
Hangar operator or an external provider. Its use may change an aircraft
without merging the tool, provider, operator and aircraft into one entity.

The analogy therefore keeps several relations distinguishable:

```text
tool owner
tool provider
tool operator
tool input
tool output
target event or target entity
resulting observable state change
provenance
```

Organizational or legal ownership and methodical source attribution answer
different questions. A third-party tool may be operated by A and affect B. A
tool output may be context, visible reference material, a measurement trace or
a new source-attributed event, depending on the declared operator and
architecture. Its role must be classified; it does not follow automatically
from the label `tool`.

A tool also does not automatically become an observed entity. It enters that
role only where the observation architecture explicitly declares its own
attributable events as part of the evaluation.

## 9. The journalist on the stairs

The origin analogy also included a journalist standing outside the Hangar on a
staircase and looking through a window. The journalist believes that they are
independently preparing a report. Unknown to them, a microphone and a small
camera have been attached to their equipment. A third party could later use
the resulting data through a technical Observer and, separately, a Controller.

This is an ethical and architectural thought experiment, not an implementation
recommendation. Its purpose is to expose role ambiguity. Depending on the
declared architecture, the journalist could simultaneously be:

- an observed entity producing attributable events;
- a carrier of observation instruments controlled by another party;
- the author of a separate report;
- an analyst of what they knowingly perceive;
- a source for a technically separate third-party Observer.

The visible person is not automatically the formal Observer. A microphone or
camera is an instrument. The party that captures and evaluates its data may
operate the technical Observer. Any later Controller requires its own declared
authority, policy and governance basis.

Technical separation does not resolve consent, transparency, purpose,
retention, access or accountability. Unknown instrumentation would raise
serious ethical and legal questions. The thought experiment shows where those
responsibilities must be made explicit; it does not legitimize covert
observation.

## 10. Many aircraft: monadic, dyadic and n-adic

A Hangar can contain many aircraft. Each can be inspected and documented in
its own state history:

```text
A -> Z_A -> IK_A
B -> Z_B -> IK_B
C -> Z_C -> IK_C
...
```

Co-presence remains monadic unless a relational question is separately
declared. The same room, staff, tools, task or time does not automatically
create relational evaluation.

A dyad requires an explicit pairing question. An n-adic observation requires
a named constellation, declared membership, separate trajectories and a
cardinality-compatible relational contract. Every aircraft and state record
remains distinguishable in an n-adic Hangar.

## 11. The Observer-side Hangar

The canonical KSODI Hangar is not a building that contains private thoughts or
hidden model states. It is an Observer-side projection, inspection and
comparison space for typed, attributable observation objects reconstructed
under compatible conditions.

Depending on the declared question, a Hangar view may contain:

- complete `Z_A(k_A)` points;
- typed incomplete Z records that are not zero-filled;
- source-local trajectories and their changes;
- monadic `IK_A` projections;
- declared windows and Sigma aggregations;
- `R_0` results and separate gate states;
- valid `IK_rel` observations after the exact compatible gate;
- compatible distributions, point clouds, corridors and drift paths.

The metaphor does not generate these objects. Every view is derived from
canonical event, identity, measurement, profile, status and provenance data.
Each distribution or comparison must declare its object type, entity,
trajectory, index, basis, status set, window rule and version.

The transient interaction room and the canonical Observer-side Hangar are also
not the same object. Contributions become observable in interaction; the
Hangar retains or reconstructs typed views from their traces. Maintenance in
the origin image belongs to separately attributable participants, tools or a
later governed action process; the Observer-side Hangar does not perform it.

## 12. Visualization as a design heuristic

Visualizations accompanied the development of several KSODI calculations. The
question was not only `Which number can be calculated?` It was also:

> What should this calculation make visible, and what would a representation
> have to preserve so that identity, typed uncertainty and layer boundaries do
> not disappear?

An imagined visualization is not a mathematical proof or empirical
validation. It can nevertheless serve as a design heuristic and semantic
consistency test:

- If A and B can be shown only by mixing them, entity or trajectory separation
  may be missing.
- If `not_selected`, `not_observable` and `not_applicable` appear only as zero,
  the representation has lost typed status.
- If an `R_0` gate looks like a resonance value, the layer boundary is blurred.
- If an `IK` projection looks like the complete state, its reduction is hidden.
- If a point cloud mixes profiles, axes, windows or pairings, visual elegance
  does not establish comparability.
- If an Observer finding and Controller action appear as one unbroken step,
  observation is being mistaken for authority to act.

Future methodological and visualization work can therefore ask of every
calculation:

```text
1. Which observation question is being answered?
2. Which mathematical object and common mathematical form are used?
3. Why is that construction appropriate for the question?
4. Which identities, statuses, bases, profiles and prerequisites apply?
5. Which visualization explains or tests the intended object?
6. What must that visualization not suggest?
7. Which alternatives remain open without becoming canonical?
8. Which empirical or theoretical validation remains unresolved?
```

This can make the mathematics more accessible across disciplines without
inventing a retrospective derivation from familiar mathematical families.

## 13. Limits of the analogy

The Hangar analogy is a bridge for understanding, not a formula or ontology.
It must not be used to claim that:

- KSODI observes private thoughts or hidden internal states;
- a shared room creates a shared state or merged mind;
- ownership automatically determines source attribution;
- tool use merges tool, provider, operator and target entity;
- co-presence creates a dyad or n-adic relation;
- an open `R_0` proves contact, coupling, resonance or causality;
- `IK_rel` merges two trajectories;
- an Observer finding authorizes the Observer to act;
- technical role separation replaces consent, privacy, law or governance.

The analogy is useful only while it preserves the same discipline as the
method: distinguishable objects, declared relations, visible transformations,
traceable tools and a separate observation and action chain.

## 14. Compact analogy map

| Hangar image | Bounded KSODI reading | Not implied |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aircraft | distinguishable entity or attributable trajectory | complete inner state |
| Maintenance record | typed source-attributed observation record, including Z where complete | the entity itself |
| Maintenance-monitor display | a declared projection such as IK | complete state or relational result |
| Shared workshop | comparison context for distinguishable objects | merged mind, entity or trajectory |
| Tool | declared instrument, service or transformation with provenance | automatic observer entity or canonical method component |
| Relational checkpoint | `R_0` under its exact contract | contact, coupling, resonance or causality |
| Dyadic comparison display | current `IK_rel` after its compatible gate | n-adic result or merged dyad |
| Observer-side Hangar view | typed points, paths, windows, distributions or clouds | private thought space or Controller |
| Maintenance decision | separate human or Controller action under authority and policy | automatic consequence of an Observer value |

## 15. Core principle

> The KSODI Hangar emerged from the image of a shared workshop in which
> distinguishable contributions can be made visible and worked on without
> merging their origin or identity. Formally, the Observer-side Hangar retains
> and compares typed, source-attributed states, trajectories, windows and
> relational projections. It observes no private thoughts, performs no
> maintenance or intervention, and grants no Controller authority by itself.
