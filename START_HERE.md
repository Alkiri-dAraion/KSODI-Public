## A Picture Before the Mathematics

Imagine an interaction as a film rather than as one isolated message. An
**event** is one frame. A **trajectory** is the ordered sequence of frames that
belong to the same distinguishable source. KSODI keeps those sources separate
before it asks any relational question.

The central terms can be read through this picture:

- **K/S/O/D/I operators:** five declared observation lenses for context,
  structure, grounding, clarity and information impulse. They describe one
  observable event under stated conditions; they do not score a person or read
  hidden intentions.
- **`Z` state vector:** one instrument panel that places five aligned
  observation records together for one source-attributed event. An unavailable
  reading remains visibly typed instead of being turned into zero. `Z` is a
  mathematical state description, not a fusion of participants.
- **Monadic, dyadic and n-adic:** these words first count how many
  distinguishable source trajectories a question concerns: one, two, or a
  declared group of more than two.
- **`IK` projection:** a deliberately compressed view of one source's `Z`
  state along a declared axis. *Monadic* means that this view still concerns
  one distinguishable source trajectory.
- **`R_0` gate:** a checkpoint before a declared downstream relational branch.
  It asks whether two or more distinguishable trajectories satisfy the exact
  gate contract required by that branch. An open gate establishes only
  methodological eligibility for that evaluation; it does not mean contact,
  agreement, coupling or resonance.
- **`IK_rel`:** a relational projection available only after its exact gate
  conditions are met. The current public definition is *dyadic*: it compares
  two explicitly paired trajectories without merging them or claiming access
  to either participant's inner state.
- **`Delta` and `Delta2`:** change from one comparable frame to the next, and
  change of that change. A declared **window** selects several frames; **Sigma**
  summarizes an eligible window inside one layer rather than creating a new
  main layer.
- **Hangar:** picture an aircraft maintenance hangar. Observable,
  source-attributed “runway traces” can be brought into one observer-side
  space, placed beside one another and inspected across windows for patterns,
  distributions or drift. The aircraft are not fused, and KSODI never enters
  a participant's imagined inner hangar. The formal boundaries are explained
  in [KSODI Hangar V350](./KSODI-Hangar_V350.md).
- **Corridor:** a declared expected region or path for comparable observations
  under one profile. Leaving a corridor is a review signal, not an automatic
  diagnosis, judgement or instruction to intervene.
- **Observer:** the separate observation layer that works only with declared,
  observable or reconstructable traces. It is not a hidden-state reader and
  does not decide, steer or enforce by itself.

These images are orientation aids. The linked versioned method files remain
authoritative for definitions, formulas, applicability and calculation.

Read the three KSODI scopes in this order:

1. **[KSODI-Light](./KSODI-Light/README.md)** — the compact, human-facing
   entry layer for reflective work within an interaction. It does not require
   an external Observer.
2. **[KSODI-Standard-Eval](./KSODI-Standard-Eval/README.md)** — the formal
   external-Observer line for one source trajectory at a time, formalized as
   `K/S/O/D/I -> Z_A(k_A) -> IK_A(k_A)`.
3. **[KSODI-Full](./KSODI-Full/README.md)** — the relational Observer extension
   beginning at `R_0`. It can ask dyadic questions about two trajectories or
   explicitly n-adic questions about more than two; the current public
   `IK_rel` projection remains strictly dyadic and requires its exact open
   dyadic `R_0` gate. Later R-family and signal-media elements assigned to
   Layers 6–8 remain staged. KSODI-Full never merges the source trajectories.
