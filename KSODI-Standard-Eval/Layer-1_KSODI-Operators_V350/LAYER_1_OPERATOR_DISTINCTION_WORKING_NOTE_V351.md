# Operator Distinction / Functional Non-Redundancy Research Working Note

Version: KSODI v3.51

This is a bounded research note. Empirical non-redundancy, mathematical
orthogonality, statistical independence and baseline completeness remain open
questions rather than released findings.

Purpose: explain why the five Layer-1 coordinates remain distinguishable
without claiming mathematical orthogonality, statistical independence or a
universal causal order.

This note preserves a wider hypothesis and question space than the canonical
operator definitions. That openness permits exploratory calculations,
counterexamples and provisional interpretations; it does not raise their
evidential status or alter the method without separate review.

## 1. Reader Orientation

KSODI observes one source-attributed event at a time. For entity or source
`A`, the complete monadic Layer-1 observation is reported in the fixed
coordinate order

```text
Z_A(k_A) = (
  K_A(k_A),
  S_A(k_A),
  O_A(k_A),
  D_A(k_A),
  I_A(k_A)
)
```

Here `k_A` is the source-local position in trajectory `A`. A global event
index `n` may locate the event in a wider stream; it does not replace
`k_A`. Relational index `j` is reserved for a later explicitly paired
evaluation through `pi(j)`.

The coordinate order `(K,S,O,D,I)` is a stable reporting convention. It is
not a claim that cognition, communication or machine processing always occurs
in that causal order.

The current v3.51 guardrail is:

> The five base operators are semantically distinguishable through their
> separate definitions, observation targets, measurement bases and profiles.
> Their functional non-redundancy has not yet been established empirically.
> They are not claimed to be mathematically orthogonal or statistically
> independent.

Overlap and correlation are allowed. Correlation is not identity, and neither
correlation nor coordinate order establishes causality.

## 2. Coordinate Questions and Boundaries

Each operator needs its own target question, basis, profile and applicability
state.

| Coordinate | Observable question | Does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `K_A(k_A)` | Are the declared expected context requirements fulfilled inside the admissible scope under the selected availability relation? | Completeness of the world, context use, grounding, answer quality or shared context between entities. |
| `S_A(k_A)` | Is observable structure, order or patterning present under the active structural profile? | Meaning, grounding, clarity or successful decoding. |
| `O_A(k_A)` | Where a source need exists, is the target visibly grounded or traceable relative to a declared admissible reference space? | Truth in itself, causal origin or shared interpretation. |
| `D_A(k_A)` | Is the observed signal distinct, dense and locally stable enough to support a continued reconstruction attempt under the active profile? | Completed reconstruction, acknowledgement, meaning or source attribution. |
| `I_A(k_A)` | Does the target contribution create an observable information difference relative to the admitted static baseline and representation profile declared for this evaluation? | Novelty alone, grounding, intent, successful contact or semantic understanding. A direct predecessor comparison is a separate source-local trajectory diagnostic, not the static I coordinate. |

A status such as `not_observable`, `not_applicable` or `not_selected` is
not an ordinary numeric value. A reduced or masked observation must be named as
such; it is not the complete canonical `Z_A(k_A)` or `IK_A(k_A)`.

## 3. Coordinate Order and Process Topologies

The same five distinctions can participate in different directed processes.

### 3.1 Sender-side formation

A sender may begin from its own context and move toward a channel-constrained
signal:

```text
K -> S -> O -> D -> I
```

This is a preferred formation topology: context is selected, structure or
convention is applied, source support may be attached where required, material
is compressed or sharpened for the channel, and a signal is emitted. A known
convention can shorten or reorder parts of this work.

### 3.2 Receiver-side reconstruction

For an unknown observed signal, the preferred iterative reconstruction
topology runs in the reverse direction:

```text
I -> D -> O -> S -> K
^                   |
|___________________|
```

The observer first needs enough observable impulse or repetition to notice a
possible signal. It then tests whether the signal is distinct enough to examine,
whether admissible sources can ground it, whether structure or convention can
be reconstructed and which context makes the result intelligible.

This is an iterative investigative preference, not a proof that every receiver
or every event executes five serial operations. Known conventions, prior
sources and parallel processing may permit shortcuts. If the signal remains
unknown, repetition can be decisive for noticing bursts, oscillation or stable
patterning and may support hypotheses about contact attempts or adversarial
patterns without proving which interpretation is correct.

Exploratory research lead: a single symbol or a short
repeated sequence may already be sufficient to flag an adversarial-pattern
hypothesis under a suitable detector. This observation has not yet been
reproduced under a declared KSODI architecture, implementation, detector
profile and validation protocol. Neither repetition nor a one-symbol flag by
itself proves an attack, hostile intent, causal origin or Controller authority.

## 4. Main Near-Edges

Near-edges are expected places of correlation or detector overlap. They are
review points, not evidence that two coordinates are identical.

### K / S

Availability of an expected format requirement under the declared K relation
belongs to `K`; observable compliance with the structural convention belongs
to `S`. If no structural requirement applies, `S` requires typed
applicability handling rather than an automatic score reduction.

### K / O

One context object can affect both coordinates under different contracts. `K`
tests fulfillment of an expected context requirement under its declared
availability relation. `O` asks whether the target is traceable against an
admissible evaluator-visible reference space where a source need exists.
Context presence does not establish grounding, and missing evaluator visibility
must not be reported as failure of the observed source.

### D / O

A clear signal can still be ungrounded, and a grounded signal can still be hard
to distinguish. If an implementation uses the same embedding model or
reference corpus for both detectors, strong correlation may be a detector
design effect. Such implementation choices belong in an implementation
companion and must not redefine the operators.

### S / O

A citation block, label or source marker may provide both visible structure and
an attribution trace. Profiles must declare whether the feature contributes to
`S`, `O` or both, and how double counting is prevented.

### S / D

Structure can improve discernibility, but structure is not discernibility. A
well-segmented unit may remain unclear; a short unsegmented pulse can remain
highly distinct.

### O / I

Grounding and signal impulse are separate. A repeated signal may carry a strong
observable impulse while its source remains unknown. A well-grounded reply may
add little change while still remaining traceable.

### D / I

Detection of an impulse precedes any claim that it can be reconstructed.
Repetition may increase local density and discernibility without adding
semantic novelty. A strong but unstable impulse can remain difficult to
examine.

## 5. Three Recurring Examples

### 5.1 Human and chatbot

A human request and a chatbot response remain separate source-attributed
events. If the chatbot responds, the exchange roles reverse for the next edge;
the entity identities do not. Each event first receives its own monadic
`K/S/O/D/I` observation and its own `Z`. A reply edge does not by itself
prove shared meaning, acknowledgement or causality.

### 5.2 Unknown Morse-like signal

An observer detects repeated pulses from a provisionally named observed source
`U`. Repetition may make `I_U(k_U)` observable before the emitting entity,
code or intent is known. `D` asks whether timing and contrast are stable
enough to continue reconstruction. `O` checks admissible known sources where
a source need exists. `S` examines pattern and possible convention; `K`
tests candidate contexts.

Even a successful pattern match does not alone prove who emitted the signal,
whether it was received as intended, whether it is hostile or whether a later
reply is an acknowledgement. SYN/ACK language is therefore only a bounded
analogy.

### 5.3 Robots

Two robots may use a shared protocol and therefore decode a signal quickly.
Their convention can reduce receiver-side reconstruction work, but it does not
collapse the five observation questions. If their distinguishable trajectories
later pass `R0`, relational coherence, geometric relation and pacing may be
examined as separate parallel branches. Equal pacing does not imply equal
geometry, and neither branch redefines a Layer-1 coordinate.

## 6. Double-Counting and Source Discipline

A feature may be visible to more than one detector. Before accepting a profile,
record:

- the source-attributed target event;
- the basis and observer visibility for each coordinate;
- applicability and missingness separately;
- which visible feature contributes to which detector;
- whether a shared detector or corpus creates artificial correlation;
- how weighting or masking avoids silent double counting;
- whether outside material is used only as a declared reference basis.

Material from outside trajectory `A` may appear in a visible reference space
where the profile permits it. That does not merge sources and does not create a
relational value. Cross-entity comparison begins only after the separately
declared `R0` gate and explicit pairing or constellation.

## 7. Interpretation Guardrail

Use this wording in orientation material:

> The KSODI base operators are semantically distinguishable through their
> separate definitions, observation targets, measurement bases and profiles.
> Functional non-redundancy remains an open empirical question. Later work may
> test operator ablation, simpler baselines, correlations, overlaps, detector
> effects and possible dependencies between comparable operator values. The
> coordinates are not claimed to be mathematically orthogonal or statistically
> independent.

Do not turn this statement into any of the following claims:

- the coordinates are proven orthogonal basis vectors;
- the coordinates are causally independent;
- equal initial weights are empirically optimal;
- one detector feature can safely be counted several times;
- a high composite value proves that every coordinate is healthy.

Initial equal weighting is an explicit normalized observation mode, not a
discovered law. Later weights or governance corridors must be selected or
learned for the declared question, application field and use case.

## 8. Later Validation Work

Later validation may examine:

- operator ablation and comparison with simpler lower-dimensional baselines;
- pairwise correlations over comparable source-local windows;
- whether correlation reflects a domain relation or detector design;
- whether shared embeddings, corpora or markers cause silent overlap;
- whether a coordinate has become too broad and absorbs another question;
- whether profiles, applicability masks or weights require adjustment;
- whether findings replicate across Human–Chatbot, signal and robot domains.

Such validation is research work. The underlying formulas may be established
mathematical tools while their KSODI composition and application remain subject
to domain validation.

### 8.1 Preserved reported research lead: FABLE orthogonality route

One reported cross-model research pass associated with the FABLE working
context worked through an exemplar as an orthogonality calculation and proposed
a possible proof route. The exact derivation, premises,
observation space and source artifact have not yet been integrated or
independently reproduced in this repository. The report is therefore preserved
as a research lead, not as proof that the five KSODI operators are orthogonal or
functionally non-redundant.

Before any stronger claim, the review must:

1. retrieve and preserve the exact exemplar and derivation;
2. reproduce every mathematical step and identify the assumptions under which
   the result holds;
3. determine whether the construction concerns semantic dimensions, formula
   vectors, detector outputs or empirical coordinate distributions;
4. verify that the architecture preserves the required separation of targets,
   bases, profiles, identities and provenance;
5. test functional non-redundancy empirically through ablation, correlation and
   comparison with simpler baselines.

Architectural separation can show that the method does not collapse the five
questions by design. It cannot by itself establish statistical orthogonality or
empirical functional non-redundancy.

### 8.2 Baseline adequacy and possible additional coordinates

The five operators remain a candidate minimal observable semantic baseline,
not a completeness theorem. Later research may ask:

- whether one coordinate is reducible to the others under a declared domain
  and profile;
- whether the five coordinates are approximately orthogonal only under some
  representations or conditions;
- whether domain-dependent weighting changes their practical contribution;
- whether an apparently missing distinction is a sixth monadic coordinate, a
  relational/process/medium complement or a latent outcome that is not
  identifiable as an operator;
- how much each coordinate contributes to reconstruction of meaning under a
  defined question and evidence regime.

No matched-case difference, correlation or additional predictive information
alone proves a new axis. Such a candidate requires its own observable object,
non-redundancy, calibration, ablation and cross-domain tests.

## 9. Research and Publication Boundary

Before public use:

- replace historical “orthogonality” wording with “semantic distinction” in
  active headings unless a separately verified mathematical claim is intended;
- use “functional non-redundancy” only as a named research question until the
  required empirical evidence exists;
- keep explicit that mathematical orthogonality, statistical independence and
  universal causal order are neither proven nor assumed;
- verify every boundary against the five current operator methods, the shared
  measurement-basis control and the identity contract;
- preserve the exact source and status of reported or cross-model leads;
- treat publication of this note as publication of open questions and
  guardrails, not as validation of orthogonality, completeness or optimality.
