## 1. Why “baseline” needs qualification

In KSODI-Light, a baseline is a simple orientation frame for noticing whether
an interaction has enough context, structure, grounding, clarity and
information for the current purpose. It is not one universal normal state of
communication, a quality score for people or a claim that every domain should
look the same.

The word can refer to several different objects:

- an **interaction baseline**: what is visibly available at a declared point or
  interval in an interaction;
- a **communication baseline**: the minimum reconstructible conditions under
  which observable contributions can be treated as communication for a
  declared question;
- a **comparison baseline**: a named reference case, earlier state, corridor or
  distribution against which later observations are compared;
- a **method baseline**: the smallest shared set of questions that KSODI-Light
  contributes before more formal Observer layers or external methods are
  added.

These meanings must not be silently exchanged. A report should say which
baseline, for which object, question, perspective, time or window it uses.

## 2. Interaction is not automatically reconstructed communication

An observable signal, event or attributable state change may support an
interaction observation. Treating that event as communication additionally
requires an explainable code, context or reconstruction relation.

Accordingly:

```text
observable interaction event
!= reconstructed communication
!= demonstrated understanding
!= demonstrated agreement
```

KSODI-Light can help a person or assistant notice missing conditions. It does
not by itself prove meaning, successful understanding, agreement, truth or
causal influence.

## 3. Are K/S/O/D/I axes?

In everyday KSODI-Light use, K/S/O/D/I are best understood as five connected
reflection dimensions or questions. Calling them “axes” can be a useful visual
metaphor, but v3.51 does not claim that they are mathematically orthogonal,
statistically independent or equally scaled.

In the formal KSODI Observer layers, the related operators have separate
definitions, evidence rules, profiles and result identities. Separate
definitions preserve distinguishability; they do not prove empirical
independence. Correlations, trade-offs or shared evidence may occur and must be
examined rather than assumed away.

## 4. How far can the five dimensions reconstruct meaning?

The five dimensions may help reconstruct observable conditions under which a
contribution becomes more or less interpretable:

- `K` asks what context is available;
- `S` asks how the contribution is organized;
- `O` asks what grounding or reference is available;
- `D` asks what can be distinguished clearly enough;
- `I` asks what information contribution or impulse is observable.

They do not exhaust semantics. Meaning may also depend on language, code,
history, embodiment, culture, role, power, media, timing, relation and domain
knowledge. KSODI therefore preserves a route from a compact orientation back
to the source material and its declared context. The baseline supports
reconstruction; it does not replace interpretation.

## 5. Weighting is a question, not a Light default

KSODI-Light has no universal weighting and no canonical total score. Which
dimension matters most can change with the task:

- emergency communication may emphasize clarity and distinguishability;
- research review may emphasize grounding and context;
- teaching may emphasize structure, context and the learner's current
  information needs;
- creative work may deliberately tolerate ambiguity while still requiring a
  usable shared frame.

A weighting is therefore a declared profile or interpretation choice. It
should identify its purpose, domain, evidence and limitations. It must not be
presented as a natural property of communication merely because it was useful
in one setting.

## 6. What observations can be derived?

KSODI-Light can support bounded observations such as:

- a needed context is missing or implicit;
- a request and response use incompatible structures;
- a claim lacks an inspectable reference for the current purpose;
- an important distinction remains unclear;
- a contribution adds little new task-relevant information;
- two passes through the same material reveal a changed orientation need.

These are prompts for clarification, comparison or deeper investigation. They
are not diagnoses of a person, automatic error labels, Controller commands or
proofs of communication quality.

Longitudinal claims require additional identity, time, profile, retention and
comparison conditions. Prompt presence alone does not prove persistent
learning, memory, internal state change or causal long-term effect.

## 7. Could there be a sixth dimension?

Yes, as an open research question. A proposed sixth dimension should not be
accepted merely because its topic is important. It should first be tested for:

1. semantic distinguishability from K/S/O/D/I;
2. evidence that it cannot be represented adequately by an existing dimension,
   profile, relation or later layer;
3. repeatable observation conditions;
4. added reconstruction value for a declared question;
5. compatibility with provenance, non-authority and return-to-evidence rules;
6. failure cases in which the proposed dimension should remain unavailable.

Time, pace, voice, relation and resonance are important examples, but they may
belong to trajectories, profiles, relational gates or later R-family views
rather than to a sixth KSODI-Light dimension. The correct location is therefore
part of the research question.

## 8. Questions for collaborative research

Contributions are welcome, especially from communication and interaction
research, education, care, software engineering, safety, linguistics and other
domains that can provide concrete counterexamples or comparison material.

Useful contributions include:

- a precise baseline object and observation question;
- cases that distinguish interaction from reconstructed communication;
- evidence for dependence or independence among K/S/O/D/I;
- domain-specific weighting hypotheses with failure cases;
- candidate sixth dimensions and evidence that existing constructs are
  insufficient;
- examples in which KSODI-Light helps orientation but does not reconstruct
  meaning adequately;
- alternative terminology that improves accessibility without erasing the
  method boundary.

A contribution should distinguish observation, inference, interpretation and
proposal. It may be submitted through the
[KSODI Public repository](https://github.com/Alkiri-dAraion/KSODI-Public) with
its source, scope and uncertainty visible.

## 9. Relation to the wider method

This note explains the KSODI-Light baseline question first. Formal operator
contracts, complete typed states, trajectories, gates and relational
calculations belong to KSODI-Standard-Eval and KSODI-Full. They may test and
refine the questions raised here; they must not be read back into
KSODI-Light as if Light already calculated those results.
